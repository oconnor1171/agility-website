What a plan looks like

Each action has a target, the reason for that target, an owner and a start month.

  1. Rebid the two largest materials suppliers

    Target
    Materials at 29% of revenue
    Owner and start
    Operations manager, month 2
    Why this target
    The business already ran at 29% in its best three months last year.

  2. Schedule crews by job size

    Target
    Direct labor at 31% of revenue
    Owner and start
    Owner, month 1
    Why this target
    The industry average, adjusted for how this business splits work between its own crews and subcontractors.

  3. Renegotiate the equipment lease at renewal

    Target
    Overhead at 15% of revenue
    Owner and start
    Owner, month 7
    Why this target
    The lease cannot change until it ends in month 7, so the target starts then.

Each action carries its monthly effect on profit and cash in the 12-month projection.

Questions owners ask

How is this different from the Profit Leak Assessment?

The assessment looks back at a year you have finished and shows where the money went. The Improvement Plan looks forward: what your business can realistically fix, by how much, by when, and who does it.

Why not just use industry averages?

An industry average describes other companies. We adjust it for how your business actually works and start from results you have already achieved, so the targets are ones you can reach.

What happens after the plan?

We check in at 90 days on every action. If you want us to track it every month, Advisory Partner does that, and your Improvement Plan fee is credited if you start within 60 days.

Is this a guarantee of results?

No. It is a management plan based on stated assumptions and reference points from published industry surveys. Actual results will differ.

My business is larger than that. Can I still get one?

Yes. With more divisions or locations, more accounts or more transactions, we quote the plan before we start.
